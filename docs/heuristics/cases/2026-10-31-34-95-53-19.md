## Behavioral heuristic candidates
1. **Multiple credential targets in a very short window**: dotenv variants, Python/Streamlit secrets, Joomla/PHP backups and WordPress configuration. Correlate across requests, not single generic `config` paths.
2. **Path normalization and traversal-like probes**: `//`, `..;`, repeated dots and nested `.env` components. Review reverse-proxy normalization and test benign traffic before promoting any broad match.
3. **User-Agent rotation and crawler impersonation**: supplied alerts contained GPTBot, Googlebot, Bravebot, DeepSeekBot, Applebot, CCBot, OAI-AdsBot and other claimed bots. User-Agent values are unverified, spoofable strings; do not attribute traffic to those services or block solely on UA.
4. **Cross-jail deduplication**: three ban notifications for the same IP and overlapping burst are **one parent incident**, preserving the three jail observations for auditing.
5. **Review exact-path indicators against existing rules first**: `/.streamlit/secrets.toml`, `/configuration.php.bak`, `/wp-config.old`, `/config/settings.py`, `/app/local_settings.py`. Do not add duplicate rules.

## Proposed regression checks (future release)
- Positive: synthetic Nginx requests to precise high-confidence secret targets.
- Positive: burst correlation groups same source, short time window and multiple jails into one incident.
- Negative: benign `/static/`, `/config/` and crawler User-Agent alone do not trigger HIGH.
- Negative: provider ASN/country and AbuseIPDB reputation alone do not prove an attack or justify a signature.
- Test escaped/normalized paths as received by Nginx, not just URL-decoded strings.
